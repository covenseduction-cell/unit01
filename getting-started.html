Getting started

Live

Where you arrive, what you carry, and how to survive your first few days in the Caeldun Isles.

Joining

Settlers III runs on Minecraft Java Edition. The server supports recent client versions, but for the best experience use the current release. While the server is in testing, the address is shared directly with players. Ask an admin for it.

When you join, the server offers you a resource pack. Accept it. It supplies the region's plants, animals and textures: bluebells, foxgloves and wild orchids, red deer, grouse, mosquitoes and whales. Without it, a lot of creatures will look like the vanilla animal they're built on (a red deer will look like a camel).

Where you arrive

New settlers arrive on a long sand beach on the island of Tirwenn in the Breadbasket, facing south over the bay. Tirwenn is the gentlest place in the world: warm, flat, fertile, and free of wolves, bears and hostile humanoids. It's the place to find your feet before you head out.

It's morning when you first arrive, so you have a full day ahead of you.

What you carry

Every new player gets a one-time starter kit:

ArmourChainmail helmet, iron chestplate, chainmail leggings, leather boots
WeaponsIron sword, bow, 16 arrows
Food16 bread, 16 apples, 16 cooked beef

That food is a balanced diet on purpose: grains, fruit and protein. See Nutrition for why that matters.

Mind the water

Your starter armour weighs 20 units. Past 30 units of worn armour (about a full iron set) you sink in water. See Weight & encumbrance.

Your rank

Everyone starts as an Apprentice, shown as [Apprentice] before your name in chat.

Useful basics

  • Inventory is kept on death. You won't lose your pack, but dying costs some nutrition.
  • /backdeath takes you back to where you last died.
  • /sethome and /home: you get one home.
  • /spawn returns you to your spawn point.
  • Teleports take 5 seconds of standing still (moving or being hit cancels them) and have a 60-second cooldown.

The full list is on the Commands page.

Your first days

  1. Look at what's around you. Every region has its own ores, crops and animals. Read Lands & regions to see what your region has and what it lacks.
  2. Get food going. Tirwenn grows almost every crop: wheat, carrots, melons and sugar cane as well as potatoes and beetroot. Most of the world grows only the last two, so farmers here have something to sell. See Farming.
  3. Find water to drink. Thirst falls much faster than hunger. Carry water bottles.
  4. Pan for gold. A bowl in a shallow gravel stream can turn up gold nuggets, and gold is money. See Panning.
  5. Build with gravity in mind. Your first house needs to hold itself up. See Gravity & building.
  6. Plan your first voyage. Sooner or later you'll need something from another island, and you can't swim there. See Ships.
Sand can't be gathered by hand

Natural beach sand drops nothing when broken, which protects the coastline. Sand comes from panning instead.
