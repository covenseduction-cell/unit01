Settlers III

A huge, slow world of Atlantic islands and highlands. Every region is rich in something and short of something else, so you'll need roads, horses, ships and trade to get anywhere.

What kind of server is this?

Settlers III takes its idea from The Settlers I and II: resources are regional and progress is slow. You won't find everything you need where you start. Iron comes from the Rainlands, emerald from the Breadbasket, diamond from the Outer Isles, and wheat will barely grow in half the world. Getting materials from where they are to where you need them is the game.

The world looks and feels like Celtic Britain: the west coast of Scotland, the Hebrides and the Highlands. Biomes are fewer and more realistic than vanilla, with Atlantic rainforest, Caledonian pinewood, machair, heather moor and peat-dark lochs. It rains a lot.

No elytra farms on day two. Slow progress, real distance and trade between regions will give us an interesting geopolitical situation, without anyone having to roleplay.

The pillars

Regional scarcity

Each land has its own ores, crops and animals. Nobody is self-sufficient. Resources →

Weight matters

Carry too much and you slow to a crawl. Wear heavy armour into deep water and you sink. Weight →

Gravity

Anything you build has to hold itself up. Timber props, arches and careful masonry keep the roof off your head. Gravity →

Ships, not swimming

The open ocean kills swimmers and small boats. Build a real ship, load it with cargo and sail it. Ships →

Nutrition

Protein, fruit and veg, grains and water. A diet of steak alone won't keep you healthy. Nutrition →

Real gold

Money is physical gold: nuggets, bars and blocks. Trade through shop keepers you set up yourself. Economy →
